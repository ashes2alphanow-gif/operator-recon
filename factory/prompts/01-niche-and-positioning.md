# Positioning pass

Run before a spec exists. The output is a decision, not an essay.

---

Title concept: **[CONCEPT]**
Lane guess: **[doctrine | technical | practice | devotional]**

Answer these in order. Short answers.

1. **Who is the reader, in one sentence, with a job and a problem in it.**
   Not a demographic. "A 41-year-old patrol supervisor whose wife has stopped
   raising things with him" beats "men interested in relationships."
2. **What does he already believe that is wrong?** If there is no wrong belief,
   there is no book, only a summary of things he agrees with.
3. **What can he do after reading that he cannot do now?** One capability.
4. **What is already on the shelf for him?** Name real titles. For each, what
   it does well and where it leaves this reader unserved.
5. **What does Chris have that no competitor can buy?** Be specific. Years,
   selection courses, deployments, K-9 work, documents he has written. If the
   honest answer is "nothing specific," the book is a commodity and should be
   killed or reassigned to a lane where his experience is load-bearing.
6. **Claim risk.** Does the book touch money, medicine, law, or physical safety?
   If yes, the lane is technical and a named professional reviewer is required
   before it can pass the compliance gate. Say who that reviewer would be.
7. **Companion.** What is the paid thing next to the book: a toolkit, a journal,
   a card deck, a course, a bulk institutional order? The paperback is the front
   door, not the business.
8. **Kill or build.** Say which. If build, state the shape: lane, unit type,
   unit count, target words per unit, trim.

Do not pad a weak concept into a book. Killing it is a result.
