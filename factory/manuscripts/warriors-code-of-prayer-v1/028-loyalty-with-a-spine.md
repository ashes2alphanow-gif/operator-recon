# Loyalty With a Spine

> The wounds of a friend are faithful, although the kisses of an enemy are profuse. -- Proverbs 27:6

The hardest thing you will ever do on a crew is tell a friend he is wrong.

Not a stranger. Not a subordinate you can correct through the chain. A man you have worked beside for nine years, whose kids know your kids, who came to your father's funeral. And he is drinking too much, or he has gotten heavy-handed on contacts, or he has started cutting corners on paperwork in a way that is going to end him.

Everybody on the crew can see it. Nobody has said anything, because saying something feels like betrayal.

That is the counterfeit. Loyalty that cannot say a hard thing is not loyalty. It is conflict avoidance wearing a friendship, and it is how good men get lost while eight guys who love them watch it happen and say nothing until the investigation.

The proverb sets it up as a contrast between two kinds of touch. A friend wounds. An enemy is generous with the pleasant stuff. The measure of which one you are dealing with is not how it feels in the moment, it is whether he will tell you something that costs him.

There is a right way to do it and most men get the delivery wrong, which is why they avoid it. Not in front of the crew. Not stacked with four other complaints you have saved up. Not as a joke with a real point in it, which is the coward's version and everybody sees through it.

One man, one time, direct, specific, with the door left open. "I am saying this because I want you to be here in five years."

And then you stay. The wound is only faithful if you are still standing there afterward.
