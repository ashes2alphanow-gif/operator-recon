# Taking the Post

> Unless Yahweh watches over the city, the watchman guards it in vain. -- Psalm 127:1

You got up. That is the part nobody claps for.

The alarm went at 1730 for a shift that starts at 1900, and you laid there for six minutes doing the arithmetic every man in this work does. How many hours of sleep. How bad the traffic. Whether the truck has fuel. Then you put your feet on the floor, and you went, and by 1845 you were standing in a briefing room holding coffee you did not taste.

That is faith. Not the feeling of it. The act of it.

Men in this line confuse faith with certainty. They think a believing man walks out the door with his chest full of assurance and a clear picture of how the night ends. That has never been true, not for you and not for anybody who wrote a word of scripture. The psalm says the watchman guards in vain without God. It does not say the watchman gets to skip the watch. He still stands it. He still checks the gate. He still walks the fence line at 0300 in the cold with a flashlight that is dimmer than it was last month.

The verse is not permission to quit. It is a statement about who is holding the outcome. You hold the post. He holds the city.

That division of labor is the most useful thing a working man can understand about prayer. Most of what wears you down is not the work. It is carrying the outcome of the work, which was never yours to carry, and which you picked up anyway somewhere around your fourth year.

So take the post. Do the checks. Log the times. And hand the outcome across before you are standing in the parking lot at the end of it wondering why you feel forty-eight years old at thirty-four.

## THE DRILL

Before you step out of the vehicle at the start of your shift, sit for ten seconds with the engine off. Say one sentence out loud: what you are responsible for tonight, and what you are not. Then get out. Ten seconds. Every shift. It is not a ritual, it is a load check.

::: prayer
God, I took the post. I will do the checks, walk the line, and answer the radio. The ending of this night is not mine to carry and I keep picking it up anyway. Take it back. Keep the people behind me asleep and ignorant of what it costs. Amen.
