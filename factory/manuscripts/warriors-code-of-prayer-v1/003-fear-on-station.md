# Fear on Station

> Be strong and courageous. Don't be afraid. Don't be dismayed, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go. -- Joshua 1:9

There is a lie in this profession that you learned early and nobody ever corrected.

The lie is that the good operators do not feel fear. You watched a senior man walk toward a vehicle at night with his hands relaxed, and you decided he felt nothing, and you decided that your own heart rate at 140 was evidence of a defect. So you stopped mentioning it. By your third year you would not have used the word out loud in a briefing room.

Ask that senior man sometime. Buy him a coffee and ask him directly what his hands were doing. He will tell you.

Courage was never the absence of the thing. It is the decision that runs on top of it. God tells Joshua to be strong and courageous three separate times in the first chapter, which is not the sort of thing you say to a man who is not afraid. You do not tell a calm man to steady up. The command is spoken directly into the fear, not instead of it.

So stop auditing your feelings for evidence that you are unfit. The metric is not what your body did. The metric is what you did while your body was doing it. Did you get out. Did you make the approach. Did you give a clear direction on the radio with a voice that other men could work off of.

The second half of that verse is the load-bearing half. Wherever you go. Not wherever you go when you feel spiritual. The parking lot of a bar at 0230 counts. The bottom of a drainage with your light off counts.

## THE DRILL

Next time your heart rate climbs, name it out loud in the truck, alone, in four words or less. "I am scared here." Then state the next action. "I am going to wait for the second unit." Naming it costs two seconds and it stops fear from making the decision for you.

::: prayer
Lord, I am afraid more than I let on, and I have spent years treating that like a flaw. You told a soldier to take courage, which means you expected him to need it. Be with me in the dark places. Steady my voice enough that the men working off it can hear the plan. Amen.
:::
