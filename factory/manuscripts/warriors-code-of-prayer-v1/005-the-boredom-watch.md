# The Boredom Watch

> Blessed are those servants whom the lord will find watching when he comes. -- Luke 12:37

Ninety-five percent of this job is nothing happening.

Nobody tells recruits that. They show you the vehicle pursuit and the rescue and the stack at the door. They do not show you the eleven hours of sitting on a dirt road watching a treeline that does nothing, while your legs cramp and you reread the same text message from your wife four times.

And here is the trap. The man who cannot hold a boring watch will manufacture something. He drives when he should sit. He goes looking to fill the hours. Half the bad outcomes in this profession come from a bored man deciding to generate activity rather than hold a position.

The parable is about servants waiting on a master who is late. The whole test in the story is the waiting. Anybody can look sharp in the first hour. The blessing goes to the ones still watching in the fourth watch of the night, which was the worst shift then and is the worst shift now.

Holding a boring watch well is a skill. It has parts.

Keep your eyes moving on a pattern instead of staring at one point until your vision fills it in with things that are not there. Change your posture on a schedule so your body does not quit before your attention does. Do not use the phone to kill the boredom, because the phone kills the watch along with it. And decide in advance what you are looking for, so your brain has a job rather than a vacuum.

The spiritual version is identical. Most of a man's life with God is not the dramatic night. It is a long flat stretch where nothing appears to be happening, and the temptation is to manufacture an experience or to quit the post.

## THE DRILL

Tonight, work one full hour of dead time on purpose. No phone. Eyes moving on a set pattern, posture change every fifteen minutes, one specific thing you are looking for. At the end, note whether the hour was harder or easier than you expected. Most men find it easier. That is the point.
