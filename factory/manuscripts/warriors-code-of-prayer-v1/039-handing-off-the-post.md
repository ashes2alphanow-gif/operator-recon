# Handing Off the Post

> Take Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the Spirit, and lay your hand on him. -- Numbers 27:18

You can tell everything about a man by how he does a relief briefing.

The bad version takes nine seconds. He is already walking. "Nothing going on, radio is acting up, see you." And the man coming on now has to rebuild the entire picture from scratch, in the dark, and will find out about the thing that matters at the worst possible time.

The good version takes four minutes and the man relieving you can work off it immediately. What happened. What is still open. What you were worried about that never developed. What the equipment is doing. Where the gaps are. Who to call.

Nobody grades the handoff. That is exactly why it tells you who somebody is.

Moses is at the end of the largest leadership assignment in the Old Testament, and he is not going to see the objective completed. He knows it. What he asks for is not an extension. He asks God to appoint a man over the people so they will not be like sheep without a shepherd, and then he lays hands on Joshua publicly so that everybody sees the authority transfer.

He does the handoff. In front of people. With no ambiguity about who is in charge afterward.

Most men do the opposite. They hold on. They stay involved after they have left, they take calls they should not take, they let the new man run a unit with the previous man's shadow in every room. That is not loyalty to the mission. It is difficulty letting go, and it costs the new man about two years.

Every post you hold is temporary. The only question is whether you hand it off clean.

## THE DRILL

Do your next relief briefing properly, even if the guy relieving you does not want it. Four minutes. Open items, what worried you, equipment status, who to call. Then go home and do not call to check on it. The second half is the hard half.

::: prayer
God, I hand things off badly because letting go feels like not caring. Teach me to brief clean, name the man, and then actually leave. Let the next one be better than I was without needing me in the room. Amen.
:::
