# What You Say at the Table

> Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one. -- Colossians 4:6

Your family is building their picture of the world out of what gets said at your table.

Think about what actually gets said there. For most men in this work it is a short list. Who was an idiot today. What the agency did wrong. A story with the hard parts sanded off. Something about money. Then silence and phones.

That is not a moral failure. It is what comes out when a man arrives at a table with no plan and eleven hours of accumulated irritation.

But it compounds. A kid who hears four years of dinner conversation learns, without anyone teaching it, that people are mostly stupid, institutions are mostly corrupt, and adults mostly complain. He will not be able to tell you where he learned it. He will just believe it at nineteen.

Paul says speech seasoned with salt. Salt is not decoration. It preserves, and it makes a thing taste like itself. Speech seasoned with salt is speech with something actually in it. It is not the same as being positive, and it is definitely not the same as being careful.

You are allowed to say hard things at your table. You should. The job is real and pretending otherwise teaches children that adults lie about their lives. The question is whether anything else ever gets said.

You already know how to do this professionally. You brief. You give a crew the situation and then you give them a next action, because you know that situation without action produces paralysis. Your family gets the situation with no next action about four nights a week.

## THE DRILL

Bring one thing to the table tomorrow. Not a lesson. One true item that is not a complaint. Something you saw, something somebody did right, a question you actually want the answer to. Ask it before anybody sits down and let it run. One item, every meal you are home for, for two weeks.

::: prayer
Lord, my table runs on complaint and my kids are learning the world from it. Give me one true thing to bring home tomorrow that is not a grievance. Let what I say at that table preserve something instead of souring it. Amen.
:::
