# The Days You Miss

> So teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom. -- Psalm 90:12

You are going to miss the game.

Not because you are a bad father. Because the schedule came out in August and the game is on a Saturday in your rotation, and the man who would have covered it is already carrying two shifts for somebody else. That is the job. It was the job when you took it.

There are two different errors men make here and they look like opposites.

The first is pretending it does not cost anything. You get good at the line. "That is the job, buddy." Delivered flat, so nobody in the house is allowed to be sad about it out loud, because you are not allowing it in yourself. The cost is real and the family knows it is real, and a father who will not name it teaches everybody to lie about how they feel.

The second error is drowning in it. The man who misses one birthday and then carries it like a felony for two years, apologizing so much that the child ends up managing the father's guilt. Kids can handle a father who is gone. They have a much harder time handling a father who needs them to say it is okay.

The psalm asks God to teach the counting of days, and the reason given is wisdom, not grief. Counting is what makes you choose. A man with unlimited days does not have to prioritize anything. A man who has counted knows that this fall has eleven Saturdays in it and he is home for four.

So handle the miss like an operator. Name it, mark it, and put something real on the other side of it.

## THE DRILL

Get the calendar out with your wife and count. How many of his games are you actually home for this season. Say the number out loud. Then pick one that you will not miss for anything short of a callout, and tell the kid which one it is, by date, so that he is waiting for a specific thing rather than hoping in general.

::: prayer
God, I am going to miss it and I cannot fix the schedule. Keep me from pretending it does not cost anything and from making my son carry my guilt about it. Teach me to count what I have got so I spend it on purpose. Amen.
:::
