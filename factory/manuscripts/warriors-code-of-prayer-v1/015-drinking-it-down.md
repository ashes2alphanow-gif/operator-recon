# Drinking It Down

> Wine is a mocker and beer is a brawler. Whoever is led astray by them is not wise. -- Proverbs 20:1

Nobody starts out drinking to stop feeling. It starts as a crew going out after a rough one, which is a good thing and does real work for men.

The drift is slow. The crew stops going and you keep going. Then it moves to the garage. Then it moves to a number you would not say out loud to your doctor. Then, somewhere in there, it stops being a social thing and becomes a tool. It is the only thing that reliably shuts the noise off after a shift, and it works every single time, which is exactly what makes it dangerous.

The proverb gives you the shape of it. A mocker and a brawler. It does not say the wine is evil. It says it deceives. It tells you it is helping while it is taking things, and the things it takes are the ones you would have said were untouchable. Sleep quality first. Then the training you stopped doing. Then patience with your kids. Then mornings.

Here is the honest measurement, the one that cuts through the arguments you have already prepared. Not how much. Whether you can stop. Take two weeks with none. If that sentence produced an immediate reason why these next two weeks are a bad window, you have your answer and you did not need the two weeks.

This is not a lecture about being a teetotaler. It is a question about who is driving.

If you cannot stop, that is not a character verdict. It is a medical situation and it has treatment. Talk to your doctor, a peer support program, or a counselor who works with people in this line of work. Stopping some of it abruptly can be genuinely dangerous, which is a reason to get help rather than a reason to keep going.

## THE DRILL

Fourteen days, none. Write the start date on paper. Tell one man who will actually ask you about it on day seven. Track what changes in sleep and in your mornings. If you cannot make it to day three, that is not a failure, that is the diagnostic result, and it is information you needed.
