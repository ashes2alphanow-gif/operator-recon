# Eyes Up

> Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful. Your adversary walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour. -- 1 Peter 5:8

Complacency does not announce itself.

It arrives as competence. You have worked this area for six years. You know which houses have dogs, which road washes out, which family is always yelling and never dangerous. So your approach gets a little flatter. You stop clearing the back of the vehicle because it has been empty four hundred times. You walk up on the passenger side out of habit instead of out of decision.

That is the shape of it. Not laziness. Familiarity.

Peter writes to men getting hunted, and his instruction is to be sober and watchful. He is not telling them to be paranoid, and there is a difference that matters. Paranoia is exhausting and it degrades your judgment. Sobriety is clear-eyed. It means you are seeing the actual situation rather than the one you have gotten used to seeing.

Every experienced man has a story about the routine call. The one that was the same as two hundred others until the moment it was not. Yours is probably a specific address that you still slow down for.

Vigilance is not a personality trait you either got issued or did not. It is a discipline, and like every discipline it decays without deliberate reset. Fitness decays in three weeks. Language skills decay in months. Attention decays in about four hours, or faster if you are hungry.

So you rebuild it. On purpose. Every shift, not once a year at in-service training.

## THE DRILL

Pick the call type you are most complacent on. The one you would describe as routine without thinking. Before your next one, say one sentence out loud in the truck naming the specific thing that could go wrong on this specific call. Not a general threat. A named one. Then handle it like the first time.

::: prayer
God, I have gotten comfortable, and comfortable is where men in my line get hurt. Keep my eyes up. Not fearful, awake. Show me the thing I have stopped seeing because I have seen it too many times. Amen.
:::
